# IC - Infection Prevention and Control

**Program:** [[Hospital/_Hospital Index|Hospital]]
**Effective Date:** 2026-07-01
**Source:** [Joint Commission Public Standards](https://publicstandards.tools.jointcommission.org/2.DOMESTIC)

## Chapter Overview

**Overview**


To help reduce the possibility of acquiring and transmitting an infection, hospitals need to establish a systematic infection prevention and control program. The design and scope of the hospital’s program are determined by the risks specific to its location, the population(s) served, and the types of services provided. The infection prevention and control activities should also be practical and reasonable to follow while aligning with law, regulation, and evidence-based guidelines. No organization wants to jeopardize a patient’s health because its infection control activities are outmoded or too confusing to practice daily. 

The standards in this chapter address the structures and activities necessary to establish and operate an effective infection prevention and control program. Every hospital, regardless of its size or the services it provides, should do the following:
- Recognize that its infection prevention and control program plays a major role in its efforts to improve patient safety and quality of care
- Demonstrate leadership’s commitment to infection prevention and control by endorsing and participating in the organization’s infection control efforts, providing resources, and encouraging improvement
- See that staff collaborate with each other when designing and implementing the infection prevention and control program
- Develop policies and procedures to guide daily infection control activities and prepare the organization to respond to outbreak
