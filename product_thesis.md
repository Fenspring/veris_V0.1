# VERIS PRODUCT THESIS

## Current Hypothesis

Healthcare organizations do not primarily have a knowledge shortage.

They have a **knowledge connection problem**.

They possess large amounts of information across systems and documents, but those sources are generally viewed independently.

The intelligence hidden between those sources is difficult to see.

---

# Hypothesis 1 — The Hospital Already Has the Data

Veris should assume that the customer already possesses much of the knowledge required to create value.

Examples include:

* Regulations
* Accreditation standards
* Policies
* Procedures
* Education
* Competencies
* Guidelines
* Internal organizational knowledge

Therefore, Veris does not need to build its moat by accumulating proprietary copies of every external knowledge source.

The customer provides the knowledge.

Veris provides the connection and intelligence layer.

---

# Hypothesis 2 — Connections Are More Valuable Than Documents

The value of Veris should increase when it can understand relationships between knowledge objects.

A document by itself provides information.

A connected set of documents can provide organizational understanding.

---

# Hypothesis 3 — Semantic Understanding Is Required

Simple keyword matching is insufficient.

Two pieces of knowledge may describe the same underlying concept using completely different terminology.

The system must be capable of recognizing semantic equivalence, related concepts, dependencies, implementation relationships, conflicts, and other meaningful relationships.

---

# Hypothesis 4 — The Graph May Be Invisible

The underlying architecture may eventually resemble a graph.

The user interface does not have to.

The user should experience:

> "Show me what connects to this."

rather than:

> "Here is a visualization of 3,000 nodes."

The complexity belongs in the system.

The insight belongs to the user.

---

# Hypothesis 5 — The Primary Value Is Emergent Intelligence

The most valuable output may not exist in any individual source.

It emerges from comparing multiple sources.

Examples:

* A regulation aligns with a policy but not a competency.
* Education teaches something that is not reflected in policy.
* Two policies describe the same process differently.
* A regulatory change potentially affects multiple organizational artifacts.
* A procedure appears to implement a requirement that is no longer current.
* A policy has dependencies that are not obvious to its owner.

These are hypotheses about where value may exist.

The system should test them.

---

# Hypothesis 6 — Grounding Creates Trust

Users should be able to understand why Veris believes two things are connected.

A relationship without evidence is weak.

A relationship with evidence is actionable.

---

# Hypothesis 7 — The Interface Should Be Simple

The product should strive for an extremely simple mental model.

Possible interaction:

**Select something → understand its connections.**

Or:

**Ask something → see the connected knowledge and evidence.**

Or:

**Something changed → see what may be affected.**

Do not assume which interaction is correct.

Test it.

---

# Hypothesis 8 — The First Killer Workflow Should Be Discovered

Do not assume the first major workflow is:

* Regulatory monitoring
* Policy search
* Gap analysis
* Compliance mapping
* Change management

Any of these may be correct.

The first workflow should be the smallest experience that proves:

> **Connecting knowledge produces intelligence that isolated knowledge sources cannot provide.**

---

# What Would Falsify the Thesis?

The thesis should be reconsidered if:

* Users do not find cross-source connections valuable.
* Most useful relationships can be produced by ordinary search.
* Semantic relationships create too many false positives.
* Users do not trust the explanations.
* Customers prefer existing systems even when connections are available.
* The cost of maintaining semantic relationships exceeds their value.

The product should be evidence-driven.

---

# Product Category Hypothesis

Current category hypothesis:

**Healthcare Knowledge Intelligence Layer**

Long-term category hypothesis:

**Healthcare Knowledge Operating System**

These are positioning hypotheses, not architectural requirements.
