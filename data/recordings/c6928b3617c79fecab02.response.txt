Before any non-emergent transfusion, informed consent must be obtained and documented, a current type and screen must be on file, and two-person verification of the blood product and patient identification is mandatory [S3]. At the bedside, two licensed staff members must independently verify the patient's name, date of birth, MRN, blood type, unit number, expiration date and any special processing requirements against the patient's armband before hanging the product [S4]. The product must be administered through an appropriate filter within 4 hours of issue from the blood bank, with vital signs taken before the transfusion, 15 minutes after initiation and on completion, and a nurse observing the first 15 minutes [S2]. If a reaction is suspected — fever, chills, urticaria, back or flank pain, hemoglobinuria or hypotension — stop the transfusion immediately and clamp the tubing at the IV site without removing IV access, keep the line open with normal saline, and notify the physician and the blood bank [S1][S6]. Do not discard the blood product or tubing: return the implicated bag with its attached tubing, the transfusion record and any co-infused IV solutions to the blood bank for inspection and clerical check [S5][S6].
