# EM - Emergency Management

**Program:** [[Hospital/_Hospital Index|Hospital]]
**Effective Date:** 2026-07-01
**Source:** [Joint Commission Public Standards](https://publicstandards.tools.jointcommission.org/2.DOMESTIC)

## Chapter Overview

**Overview**

Hospitals will always be at the forefront of emergencies and disaster incidents such as acts of terrorism or cybersecurity attacks; natural disasters such as fires, floods, or tornadoes; or new viruses, and the response must be adapted quickly to the situation. Emergencies or disaster incidents often occur rapidly and without notice. Some may unfold on a timeline that allows for advance preparation. Others may linger for an extended period, as was the case during the COVID-19 pandemic. The structure of the emergency management program should be designed to respond to any type of emergency (all-hazards approach) because of the wide array of possible emergencies and the impossibility of predicting all emergencies that could occur at an organization. Emergency management is conducted by hospitals both as individual entities and integrated participants in the larger emergency response community.

**About This Chapter**


The “Emergency Management” (EM) chapter is organized sequentially from program development to program evaluation to assist hospitals in planning and preparing their responses to the effects of potential emergencies or disaster incidents. An emergency management program is based on the four phases of emergency management (mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery) and uses a proactive all-hazards approach to develop a framework for the program (described in EM.09.01.01). The concept of “all-hazards” means preparing for all types of threats or hazards the hospital may encounter and the impacts those would have on the hospital’s ability to operate and provide services it considers essential or critical.

The all-hazards risk assessment, known as a hazard vulnerability analysis (HVA) (described in EM.11.01.01), is designed to assist hospitals in gaining a realistic understanding of their vulnerabilities and prioritizing them to help mitigate and respond to emergencies. The hospital considers its prioritized hazards identified as part of its HVA when planning and developing a comprehensive emergency operations plan (EOP) (described in EM.12.01.01). The EOP includes supporting policies and procedures that provide guidance to staff and volunteers on actions to take during emergency or disaster incidents. These response procedures include managing the following six critical areas (described in EM.12.02.01 through EM.12.02.11) regardless of the cause(s) of an emergency:

- Communications
- Staffing
- Patient care and clinical support 
- Safety and security
- Resources and assets
- Utilities

The EOP is more than a mere paper plan. For staff to understand the EOP and related policies and procedures, education and training must be provided so that staff become familiar with their roles and responsibilities during an emergency and acquire the skills necessary to perform assigned tasks (described in EM.15.01.01). Conducting emergency exercises (described in EM.16.01.01) provides a means to validate the EOP, checklists, and response procedures and to evaluate the skills of staff. 

The development of the emergency management standards and elements of performance in this chapter are intended to guide hospitals in their efforts to develop and maintain a viable all-hazards emergency management program, EOP, and policies and procedures. When disasters threaten or strike a community, the hospital is prepared to take immediate action to deal with and respond quickly to the problem. Because of the unpredictable nature of emergencies, the hospital must evaluate the effectiveness of its EOP (described in EM.17.01.01) at least every two years to make certain that it has considered all actions it will take to protect itself, patients, staff, and the community, along with an evaluation of the resources the hospital has or can obtain
