# EM.12.02.09

The hospital has a plan for managing resources and assets during an emergency or disaster incident.
Note: The hospital considers its prioritized hazards identified as part of its hazard vulnerability analysis when developing a plan for resources and assets.

## Introduction


Resource utilization and asset acquisition occurs systemically every day in health care organizations. Under normal operating conditions, a hospital can accurately predict its supply and demand for things such as water and oxygen based on its rate of consumption. Managing resources and assets focuses on those resources that would be critically needed in an incident response in order to remain operational. Pre-incident planning is based upon existing operational plans and provides the opportunity to identify what resources are involved and what equipment would be needed based on the hospital’s prioritized risks as determined in its hazard vulnerability analysis. Resource planning for shortages includes ways to obtain, ration, or replenish resources; how far the hospital can stretch those resources; and determining the length of hours or days the hospital can remain operational. Most hospitals rely on their established contractual agreements (memorandum of understanding) and expect their vendors will continue to provide for the delivery of critically needed resources, but this may not be the case in an emergency, especially when other health care systems are competing for the same resources. Hospitals should consider planning for alternative ways to obtain resources. Hospitals that work closely with their regional health care coalitions or regional health care partners can establish ways to share critically needed resources, including beds, staff, and other supplies.      

Testing of resources and assets during emergency management exercises allows participants to identify gaps or weaknesses and provides opportunities to make improvements. Failure to recognize and plan for challenges to acquire, ration, or replenish critical resources may result in the inability to maintain functional operations, resulting in partial or even full evacuation. (For testing requirements, refer to Standard EM.16.01.01) 

## Rationale

Access to resources can often be difficult when the needs in a community or region are greater than locally available resources; therefore, the hospital’s plan includes continual assessment on how to obtain, allocate, mobilize, replenish, and conserve its resources and assets during and after an emergency or disaster incident.  

## Elements of Performance

### EP 1

The hospital’s plan for managing its resources and assets describes in writing how it will document, track, monitor, and locate the following resources (on-site and off-site inventories) and assets during and after an emergency or disaster incident: 
- Medications and related supplies
- Medical/surgical supplies
- Medical gases, including oxygen and supplies
- Potable or bottled water and nutrition
- Non-potable water
- Laboratory equipment and supplies
- Personal protective equipment
- Fuel for operations
- Equipment and nonmedical supplies to sustain operations 
Note: The hospital should be aware of the resources and assets it has readily available and what resources and assets may be quickly depleted depending on the type of emergency or disaster incident.

**Attributes:** ESP-1, Documentation

### EP 2

The hospital’s plan for managing its resources and assets describes in writing how it will obtain, allocate, mobilize, replenish, and conserve its resources and assets during and after an emergency or disaster incident, including the following: 
- If part of a health care system, coordinating within the system to request resources
- Coordinating with local supply chains or vendors  
- Coordinating with local, state, or federal agencies for additional resources  
- Coordinating with regional health care coalitions for additional resources  
- Managing donations (such as food, water, equipment, materials) 
Note: High priority should be given to resources that are known to deplete quickly and are extremely competitive to acquire and replenish (such as fuel, oxygen, personal protective equipment, ventilators, intravenous fluids, antiviral and antibiotic medications).

**Attributes:** Documentation, ESP-1
