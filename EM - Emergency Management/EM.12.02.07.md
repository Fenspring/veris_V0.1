# EM.12.02.07

The hospital has a plan for safety and security measures to take during an emergency or disaster incident.
Note: The hospital considers its prioritized hazards identified as part of its hazard vulnerability analysis when developing a plan for safety and security.

## Introduction


A coordinated response with public safety agencies and other community response entities is essential for protecting the safety and health of all staff, volunteers, patients, and visitors during an emergency or disaster incident. The plan for safety and security includes addressing situations that may require heightening security measures; tracking of and accounting for staff, volunteers, patients, and visitors; and controlling and containing exposures to hazardous materials. (For additional requirements related to safety and security measures, refer to Standard NPG.03.02.05)

Testing safety and security measures during emergency management exercises allows participants to identify gaps or weaknesses and provides opportunities to make improvements. The hospital can quickly become overwhelmed by rapidly unfolding incidents, such as civil unrest, natural disasters, or other escalating incidents. Failure to recognize and plan for these may result in the inability to provide the essential services needed for the preservation of safety and security (from both accidental and intentional harm) for all those within the facility. (For testing requirements, refer to Standard EM.16.01.01

## Rationale

Emergencies and disasters often create new and rapidly changing safety and security concerns. An emergency response plan should include the possible need for heightening security measures; tracking of and accounting for staff, patients, and visitors; and minimizing exposures to hazards. Safety and security measures include partnering with community security agencies (for example, police, sheriff, National Guard) and coordinating security activities that may be outside the span of control of the hospital’s security team. (For additional requirements related to safety and security measures, refer to Standard NPG.03.02.05)

## Elements of Performance

### EP 2

The hospital’s plan for safety and security measures includes a system to track the location of its on-duty staff and volunteers and patients when sheltered in place, relocated, or evacuated. If on-duty staff and volunteers and patients are relocated during an emergency, the hospital documents the specific name and location of the receiving facility or evacuation location.
Note: Examples of systems used for tracking purposes include the use of established technology or tracking systems or taking head counts at defined intervals.
