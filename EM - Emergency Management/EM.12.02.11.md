# EM.12.02.11

The hospital has a plan for managing essential or critical utilities during an emergency or disaster incident.
Note: The hospital considers its prioritized hazards identified as part of its hazard vulnerability analysis when developing a plan for utilities management. 

## Introduction


Different types of emergencies can have a detrimental impact on a hospital’s utility system(s), including partial or total loss of the system(s). Based on the risks identified in the hazard vulnerability analysis, hospitals should have a plan for managing utilities before, during, and after an emergency or disaster incident. When developing the plan, hospitals should begin by identifying those utilities or utility systems that would be considered essential or critical to maintain during an emergency or disaster incident. The utilities management plan addresses how the hospital will continue to provide or maintain its utility systems if one or more systems were to be impacted by a catastrophic event. The plan includes alternate ways to provide for those systems that are identified as essential or critical to remain operational. 

Testing the utility systems for potential failures will be needed during emergency management exercises, which allows participants to identify gaps and weaknesses and provides opportunities to make improvements. In a real event, a partial or complete loss of utility systems may result in the inability to maintain functional operations, resulting in partial or full evacuation. (For the emergency operations plan’s testing requirements, refer to Standard EM.16.01.01) 

## Rationale

Emergencies or disasters often have a detrimental impact on a hospital’s utility system(s), including loss of the system(s). The list of essential or critical systems that could potentially fail during an emergency can range from heating, ventilation, and air conditioning; network connectivity; and refrigeration equipment. The hospital must be prepared with alternate ways for providing essential or critical systems to maintain functional operations should one or more systems fail.

## Elements of Performance

### EP 1

The hospital’s plan for managing utilities describes in writing the utility systems that it considers as essential or critical to provide care, treatment, and services.
Note: Essential or critical utilities to consider may include systems for electrical distribution; emergency power; vertical and horizontal transport; heating, ventilation, and air conditioning; plumbing and steam boilers; medical gas; medical/surgical vacuum; and network or communication systems.

**Attributes:** Documentation, ESP-1

### EP 2

The hospital’s plan for managing utilities describes in writing how it will continue to maintain essential or critical utility systems if one or more are impacted during an emergency or disaster incident.

**Attributes:** ESP-1, Documentation

**Crosswalk:** §482.15(e), §482.15(e)

### EP 3

The hospital’s plan for managing utilities describes in writing alternative means for providing essential or critical utilities, such as water supply, emergency power supply systems, fuel storage tanks, and emergency generators.

**Attributes:** Documentation, ESP-1

### EP 4

The hospital’s plan for managing utilities includes alternate sources for maintaining energy to the following:
- Temperatures to protect patient health and safety and for the safe and sanitary storage of provisions
- Emergency lighting
- Fire detection, extinguishing, and alarm systems
- Sewage and waste disposal
Note: It is important for hospitals to consider alternative means for maintaining temperatures at a level that protects the health and safety of all persons within the facility.  For example, when safe temperature levels cannot be maintained, the hospital considers partial or full evacuation or closure.

**Attributes:** ESP-1, Documentation

**Crosswalk:** §482.15(b)(1)(ii)(D), §482.15(b)(1)(ii)(D), §482.15(b)(1)(ii)(B), §482.15(b)(1)(ii)(B), §482.15(b)(1)(ii)(A), §482.15(b)(1)(ii)(A)
